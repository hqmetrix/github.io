Case study

From EMR to QRDA I, wellness visits and diagnoses, pre-visit profiles, chase lists and shared savings

One patient-level data flow that feeds quality reporting, pre-visit care teams and the ACO's financial results.

Case Studies · Anonymized · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Case Studies

Problem

Quality reporting, wellness visit outreach and risk documentation each had their own data pull and their own lists. Care teams received long, overlapping lists with no priority, and quality results were calculated separately from the lists they were meant to improve.

Required Data

  • Patient-level clinical data from each EMR (QRDA I where available, otherwise extracts)
  • Annual wellness visit status and dates
  • Diagnoses from encounters, plus prior-year diagnoses for recapture review
  • Open quality gaps by measure
  • Upcoming appointments and attribution

FHIR Sources

  • Patient and Encounter for the roster and visit history
  • Condition for diagnoses
  • Observation and Procedure for measure evidence
  • Appointment for upcoming visits
  • Coverage or attribution data for the ACO's assigned beneficiaries

QRDA Generation

Patient-level files (QRDA I where the EMR provides them, extraction where it does not) are the single source. Measure results come from that one calculation, aggregate files for CMS are built from it, and the same patient rows feed the care-team views.

Validation

  • Gap logic tested against the measure specification on sample patients.
  • Diagnosis suggestions reviewed by clinicians for clinical support before they appear on a profile.
  • Chase list de-duplicated against completed visits and scheduled appointments.
  • Month-over-month check that closed gaps match chart evidence.

Submission

  1. Build a pre-visit profile per member: open care gaps, wellness visit status, diagnoses to review.
  2. Create a chase list ranked by upcoming visit, gap value and effort.
  3. Care teams work the list and record outcomes in the EMR as structured data.
  4. Next data refresh updates measure results.
  5. Final measure data goes to CMS in the submission window, and quality and risk results feed the ACO's shared savings picture.
ComplianceA suspected diagnosis is a prompt to evaluate the patient, not a diagnosis. Document only conditions that a clinician has assessed and supported in the record.

Common Errors

  • Gaps closed in conversation but never recorded as structured data
  • Chase lists that repeat patients already seen
  • Diagnosis lists treated as billable without clinical assessment
  • Outreach that ignores upcoming appointments
  • Quality numbers and list numbers coming from different pulls and disagreeing

At a glance

  • Setting: ACO with a care management team
  • Flow: EMR, patient data, pre-visit profile, chase list, results
  • Principle: every gap is closed with documentation of real care
  • Details are generalized. No client names, and no results are claimed that are not described here.
