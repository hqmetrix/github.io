What are Star Ratings measures?

A short tour of the measures behind Medicare Advantage and Part D ratings, and how their weights work.

Education Center · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Education Center

What they are

Each year CMS rates Medicare Advantage and Part D plans from one to five stars. The ratings feed into plan payments and what members see when they compare plans. Each rating is built from individual measures that cover screenings and chronic-condition care, member experience, complaints, access and drug safety.

The 2026 weights

Each measure carries a weight, which is how many times its star counts in the weighted average that becomes the overall rating. For the 2026 Star Ratings:

Measure typeWeight
Process1
Outcome1 for Improving or Maintaining Physical and Mental Health (new for 2026); 3 for Plan All-Cause Readmissions
Intermediate outcome3
Patient experience and complaints2
Access2
Improvement5

Patient experience, complaints and access measures were weighted 4 in earlier years; the reduction to 2 took effect with the 2026 Star Ratings. CMS's contract year 2027 final rule, issued in 2026, makes further changes, so check the Technical Notes for the rating year you care about.

Why a weight of 3 matters more than it looks

A one-star change on a weight-3 measure moves the overall rating three times as much as the same change on a weight-1 measure. That is why plans put effort into blood pressure control, blood sugar control and Part D adherence, and why provider groups with delegated care management are often asked to focus on them.

Domains and cut points

Measures are grouped into domains. The Part C domains include Staying Healthy; Managing Chronic Conditions; Member Experience with the Health Plan; and Member Complaints and Changes in the Health Plan's Performance. Part D has four: Drug Plan Customer Service; Member Complaints and Changes in the Drug Plan's Performance; Member Experience with the Drug Plan; and Drug Safety and Accuracy of Drug Pricing.

CMS sets cut points that turn a measure's rate into one to five stars, and publishes them each year in the Technical Notes. The improvement measure compares a contract's measure scores with the previous year's. Because cut points and measure sets change, a rate that earned a given rating one year may not earn it the next.

What provider groups can do

  • Ask each plan for its list of Stars measures that apply to your patients, with the data it needs from you.
  • Make sure care delivered is captured in claims or supplemental data so the plan gets credit.
  • Prioritize weight-3 measures and members close to the threshold.
  • Use monthly gap lists, not an end-of-year push.

At a glance

  • CMS publishes Part C and Part D Star Ratings every year
  • Part C and Part D measures are weighted by type
  • Weights run from 1 (process) to 5 (improvement)
  • Cut points and weights change, so read each year's Technical Notes
