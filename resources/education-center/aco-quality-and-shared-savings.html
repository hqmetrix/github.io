How ACO quality scores affect shared savings

In the Medicare Shared Savings Program, quality does not sit beside the money. It sets how much of the savings an ACO keeps.

Education Center · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Education Center

The role of quality

An ACO that beats its financial benchmark by enough to clear its minimum savings rate does not automatically keep the maximum share. Quality performance decides how much it keeps. In two-sided models, quality also affects losses: the higher the quality score, the lower the shared-loss rate.

The quality performance standard

The quality performance standard is the minimum quality an ACO must reach to share in savings at the maximum rate available for its track. Meeting it also lets an ENHANCED-track ACO avoid maximum shared losses. For PY 2026, CMS lists three ways to meet it:

  1. Score at or above the 40th percentile of MIPS quality performance category scores. For PY 2026 that value is 73.85, down from 76.70 in 2025.
  2. Report all five eCQMs, MIPS CQMs or Medicare CQMs with data completeness, and earn the reporting incentive by reaching the 10th percentile of the benchmark on at least one outcome measure and the 40th percentile on another measure.
  3. For ACOs in their first performance year of their first agreement period: report the APP Plus set, meet data completeness, administer the CAHPS for MIPS Survey, and receive a MIPS quality score.

The alternative standard

An ACO that misses the standard can still share savings at a reduced rate. It must report the APP Plus quality data and reach at least the 10th percentile of the benchmark on at least one of the four outcome measures. Its quality score is then multiplied by the track's maximum sharing rate to set its final rate.

What the money looks like

This example is illustrative and ignores caps, sequestration and the details of minimum savings rates. Suppose an ACO in the ENHANCED track, whose maximum sharing rate is 75%, generates $2.0 million in savings that clears its minimum savings rate.

The PY 2026 measure set

ACOs report the APP Plus quality measure set. For PY 2026 it has eight measures:

CMS marks the four outcome and intermediate outcome measures as outcome measures for the reporting incentive and the alternative standard. PY 2026 is the last year ACOs can choose MIPS CQMs as a collection type; confirm the final CY 2027 rules for later years.

What ACO teams can do

  • Check data completeness monthly, not in March. Missing completeness can remove a pathway.
  • Focus first on the outcome measures, because the alternative pathway depends on them.
  • Validate denominators and exclusions across every participating practice's EHR.
  • Track performance against the prior year's benchmark during the year, not after.
  • Confirm the survey and claims-based measures with your vendors early, since you cannot influence them late in the year.

At a glance

  • Meet the quality performance standard to share at the track's maximum rate
  • Miss it, and an alternative standard can still earn a reduced, scaled rate
  • In two-sided tracks, higher quality also lowers shared losses
  • For PY 2026 the standard is a quality score of 73.85
