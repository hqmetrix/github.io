Complete Guide to Multi-EHR MSSP ACO Data Submission for 2026

Your options, the 75% completeness requirement, data accuracy, why it is hard, how ACOs are approaching it, and what is coming next.

Education Center · Last checked November 17, 2026

The gist

In one paragraphTo share in savings, a Shared Savings Program ACO has to report the APP Plus quality measure set. Five of the PY 2026 measures come from clinical data, and the ACO can report each through eCQMs, MIPS CQMs or Medicare CQMs. Whatever route it picks, the ACO must cover at least 75% of eligible patients for every measure it submits, and the numbers have to be right. When participating practices run different EHRs, the work is pulling the data together, removing duplicates, proving completeness and checking accuracy before the March deadline.
  • Options: three collection types in 2026, and a fourth (Medicare eCQMs) from 2027.
  • Completeness: 75% per measure, counted across every participating practice.
  • Accuracy: one submission per measure counts, so errors in the winning route decide the score.
  • Complexity: different EHRs, different patient populations, and no tidy way to combine them.
  • Approach: monitor monthly and validate independently, not in March.
  • Future: new options, flat benchmarks and a clear push toward digital reporting.

What ACOs have to submit

ACOs report the APP Plus quality measure set. For PY 2026 the ACO reports five clinical measures (diabetes glycemic status greater than 9%, breast cancer screening, colorectal cancer screening, depression screening with follow-up, and controlling high blood pressure). The rest of the set is the CAHPS for MIPS survey, which a certified survey vendor submits, and administrative claims measures that CMS calculates (see Table 1 in the CMS PY 2026 document for the full list). Claims measures and CAHPS do not appear during the submission period. The companion article on ACO quality and shared savings explains how these scores affect the money.

Quality data is submitted at the ACO level. Participant TINs do not report APP Plus quality measures themselves and cannot see the quality data the ACO submits. That puts the whole aggregation job on the ACO or its vendor.

Measures can be reported through a mix of collection types, and the same measure can be reported through more than one. When that happens, CMS counts only the collection type with the most achievement points for that measure. In CMS's own example, a blood pressure eCQM worth 6.1 points loses to a MIPS CQM worth 7.5, and the eCQM contributes nothing.

How the data gets to CMS

  • An APM Entity staff user or Security Official uploads a QPP JSON file or a QRDA III file on the QPP site.
  • A third-party intermediary can submit on the ACO's behalf, and the ACO signs in during the window to review what was submitted.
  • The program name must be set to APP Plus. Without it, the data defaults to traditional MIPS.
  • For PY 2025, CMS required a certified EHR technology ID for eCQM submissions. Confirm the current requirement before you build your files.
  • The submission period for CY 2026 data is expected to run January 4 to March 31, 2027, with the deadline at 8 p.m. ET on the last day, as in prior years. Data cannot be corrected after it closes.

The 75% data completeness requirement

Each submitted measure must meet a data completeness requirement of 75%. In plain terms, at least three out of four of the patients who are eligible for the measure have to be included in what you submit. The requirement applies per measure, not to the ACO as a whole, and a measure that fails it does not count as reported.

For eCQMs, MIPS CQMs and the all-payer reporting that comes with them, eligible patients means everyone who interacts with any ACO provider, not only assigned beneficiaries. That is why a single missing practice can sink a measure.

In the made-up example above, losing Practice D (90%) or Practice C (80%) still clears the line, but losing Practice B (70%) fails the measure. CMS refers to its APM Performance Pathway scoring guide for the exact calculation, so confirm the details there.

  • Check completeness for every measure and every practice each month, not once a year.
  • Treat an unmapped local code or a missing file as a completeness problem, not only a data-quality one.
  • CMS has described letting ACOs exclude certain participant TINs from quality reporting if the remaining TINs still cover at least 95% of assigned beneficiaries, for example a TIN on specialty-focused certified technology that does not support APP Plus measures. The ACO must still meet the 75% requirement for each measure. Confirm the final status of that policy before relying on it.

Data accuracy

Completeness asks whether you have the patients. Accuracy asks whether you counted them correctly. A submission can be 100% complete and still be wrong if the denominator is inflated, exclusions are missed or a numerator service is not credited.

  • Because only the best-scoring collection type counts per measure, an error in the route that wins matters more than errors in the others.
  • Duplicate patients across practices inflate denominators. Identity matching across EHRs is the single biggest accuracy lever.
  • Exclusion and exception reasons recorded in free text or local codes are invisible to measure logic.
  • Measure versions and value sets differ by EHR vendor and release; one practice on last year's version distorts the roll-up.
  • Look-back windows and measurement-period boundaries are applied differently by different systems.
  • Native EHR calculators vary in quality. Verify them against an independent calculation on the same patients before you trust them.

A reliable accuracy check is simple to describe: take a sample of patients from each practice, trace each from the source record to the final measure result, and explain every difference. Keep those traces. They are your audit trail and your answer if a number is questioned.

Why this is so complex

  1. Each participating practice can run a different EHR with a different data model, and the ACO needs one consistent answer.
  2. Quality is submitted at the ACO level, but the data lives at practice level and participant TINs do not report.
  3. The patient populations differ by collection type: all payers for eCQMs and MIPS CQMs, Medicare fee-for-service for Medicare CQMs.
  4. Technology has not caught up. NAACOS says current technology lacks support for extraction, aggregation and deduplication across practices and EHRs.
  5. Vendors may be unable to produce patient-level QRDA files, may charge significant fees for them, or may refuse to support required measures such as breast cancer screening.
  6. The measures were built to assess individual clinicians and practices, especially in primary care, so ACOs with many specialists are judged on measures that do not fit their care.
  7. Measure logic, value sets and rules change every year, and the ACO has to track the changes across every EHR.
  8. Several things happen on different clocks: survey vendor submission, CMS claims measures, practice data pulls and the March upload.

How ACOs are approaching it

The common thread is moving the hard work earlier and making it repeatable:

  1. InventoryList every participating TIN, its EHR, version, hosting arrangement and what each can export.
  2. Pick routes by measureDecide, measure by measure, which collection type is likely to score best and which practices can support it.
  3. Centralize identityMatch patients across practices so one person is counted once.
  4. Calculate once, check twiceRun one consistent calculation, then compare it with each EHR's own output.
  5. Test uploads earlyBuild and test the JSON or QRDA III files well before the window opens.
  6. Share the scorecardReturn practice-level completeness and gap lists to the practices monthly.

Some ACOs also adjust their networks. NAACOS reports that many ACOs are dropping specialty practices because the measures do not fit them. That is a business decision with quality-reporting consequences, so make it with the data in hand.

Why it may get more challenging

CMS's final CY 2027 rule gives ACOs more options and more to manage. What is finalized, from CMS's own fact sheet:

  • A new Medicare eCQM collection type from CY 2027, available only to ACOs reporting APP Plus, covering the ACO's assigned beneficiaries with flat benchmarks. ACOs that choose it are not eligible for the Complex Organization Adjustment or the eCQM/MIPS CQM reporting incentive. The patient population changes, so completeness, case volume and scoring all need re-testing.
  • Flat benchmarks for all Medicare CQM measures from CY 2027, and applied retroactively to CY 2026 for Quality IDs 001, 134 and 236. Targets will not look like the historical benchmarks ACOs planned against.
  • MIPS CQMs stay available for ACOs with no end year, and the reporting incentive is extended. CMS's proposal had anticipated sunsetting them from PY 2030, when FHIR-based reporting would become mandatory. That was not finalized, but the direction toward digital, API-based reporting is clear.
  • A PY 2027 measure set of eight measures with substantive changes to three of them. The fact sheet's text on two planned measures (Quality IDs 305 and 493) is inconsistent, so verify the final list.
  • Modified certified EHR technology requirements for Shared Savings Program ACOs. The details are in CMS's Shared Savings Program fact sheet, which you should read before 2027.

Beyond the rules, the structural pressures remain: more practices and EHRs as ACOs grow, vendor dependence and cost, and measures that fit primary care better than specialty care. ACOs that build a repeatable extraction, identity and validation process now will find each change cheaper to absorb.

Where to start

  1. Build the TIN and EHR inventory this month.
  2. Run a completeness check for every 2026 measure on data through the latest month.
  3. Compare each practice's native eCQM results with an independent calculation on a sample.
  4. Decide which routes (eCQM, MIPS CQM, Medicare CQM) you will use for each measure, and what you will do if one fails.
  5. Schedule test uploads for January, and put the March deadline and your internal cutoffs on the committee calendar.

At a glance

  • ACOs report the APP Plus measure set through one or more collection types
  • At least 75% of eligible patients must be included for each submitted measure
  • Only one collection type counts per measure: the one with the most points
  • Multiple EHRs make aggregation, deduplication and accuracy the hard part
  • The final CY 2027 rule adds options and raises the stakes on data
