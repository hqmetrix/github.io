Choosing between traditional MIPS and an MVP

What differs, what is changing, and the questions that settle it for your practice.

Education Center · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Education Center

Where things stand

Clinicians can report to the Merit-based Incentive Payment System through traditional MIPS or through a MIPS Value Pathway (MVP). Clinicians in a MIPS APM, such as many ACO participants, report through the APM Performance Pathway (APP) instead.

CMS's CY 2027 final rule sets the timeline: MVPs stay optional through the CY 2030 performance period, traditional MIPS ends after that period, and from the CY 2031 performance period clinicians must report an MVP unless they are eligible to report the APP. CMS had proposed ending traditional MIPS after CY 2028 and changed that in the final rule. Thirty MVPs are available for 2027, including new ones for diabetic disease, hypertension and hospitalist care.

Both paths score the same four performance categories. For 2026 the weights are Quality 30%, Cost 30%, Improvement Activities 15% and Promoting Interoperability 25%, and the performance threshold is 75 points.

Dates to knowFor the 2026 performance year, data submission runs January 4 to March 31, 2027, and MVP registration closes November 30, 2026. Confirm both on qpp.cms.gov, since registration windows and system dates can shift.

Five questions that decide it

  1. Is there an MVP that fits what your clinicians actually treat? If not, traditional MIPS may still be the better fit for now.
  2. Do you already have clean data for the MVP's measures? An MVP is only easier if the data is there.
  3. Do you want to report as a group, as individuals, or as subgroups by specialty? Registration asks which.
  4. Does your registry or QCDR support the MVP you are considering? Registries have until one year after an MVP is finalized to support it.
  5. Are you an ACO participant? If so, the APP likely applies and this decision may not.

A reasonable approach

Because traditional MIPS will end, most practices benefit from testing an MVP before they have to switch. A common plan is to model last year's data under the MVP that fits best, compare the result with traditional MIPS, and register for the MVP only when the data supports it. Registration each year closes November 30, so decide by early fall.

At a glance

  • Traditional MIPS ends after the 2030 performance period
  • MVPs are optional through 2030, then required for most clinicians
  • MVP registration closes November 30 each year
  • ACO participants usually report through the APP, not MIPS
