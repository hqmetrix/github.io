QRDA I vs QRDA III: which one, and when

One describes a patient, the other describes a population. Here is how that changes your workflow.

Blog · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Blog

The difference

QRDA Category IQRDA Category III
ContainsQuality data for one patient for one or more eCQMsQuality data for a set of patients for one or more eCQMs
LevelPatient-levelAggregate
Typical CMS useHospital programsEligible clinician and ACO programs
Contains PHIYes, one patient per fileSummary counts, no patient detail
Main benefitTraceable to a patient; good for audit and gap listsCompact; matches how clinician programs score

Both standards are maintained by the HL7 Clinical Quality Information Workgroup and constrain CDA Release 2 for exchanging eCQM data. Certified EHR technology is expected to support both categories.

Why this matters for an ACO

  • A QRDA III from each practice gives you totals but not the patients, so you cannot remove a patient who appears at two practices.
  • Patient-level data lets you deduplicate before you aggregate, which is why many multi-EHR ACOs collect patient-level data (QRDA I or direct extraction) and build the aggregate themselves.
  • Patient-level files are also the raw material for gap lists and chase lists.
  • Some vendors do not produce patient-level files at all, or charge extra for them. Ask early.
Rule of thumbSubmit aggregate, collect patient-level. The submission can be aggregate, but your working data should let you answer "which patients?"

When a QRDA I is not available

If an EHR cannot produce QRDA I, you can extract clinical data directly or use C-CDA documents. See the case study on producing patient-level data without QRDA I.

At a glance

  • QRDA I: one patient, one or more measures
  • QRDA III: aggregate results for a set of patients
  • Both are based on HL7 CDA Release 2
  • Clinician and ACO programs use QRDA III or QPP JSON
