Qualified Registry Requirements: what CMS expects

What a third party must do to submit MIPS and APP data for clinicians and ACOs, and what to ask any vendor.

Blog · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Blog

What a qualified registry is

A qualified registry is a third-party intermediary that collects clinical data from clinicians, groups, virtual groups or APM Entities and submits it to CMS. The requirements sit in 42 CFR 414.1400.

Requirements to know

  • Self-nomination happens every year. For the 2026 performance period the window ran from July 1 to 8 p.m. ET on September 2, 2025.
  • A data validation plan is submitted at self-nomination for CMS approval and cannot be changed once approved.
  • The registry must conduct data validation before submitting data for the payment year, on data for each performance period.
  • A data validation execution report documents the results, including calculation issues found and how they were fixed. A late report can lead to remedial action or termination.
  • Registries must support subgroup reporting beginning with the CY 2023 performance period.

That list is a summary, not the full regulation. Read the current rule and the year's self-nomination fact sheet.

Questions to ask any registry

  1. Can I see your approved data validation plan and last year's execution summary?
  2. Which collection types and measures will you support for my ACO this year?
  3. Can you produce patient-level data, and at what cost?
  4. How do you handle duplicate patients across practices?
  5. What is your support path and turnaround when something fails near the deadline?
  6. What do I see during the submission window, and who can correct what?
Why it mattersThe registry is accountable to CMS for validation, but you are accountable for your ACO's result. Keep your own checks.

At a glance

  • Self-nomination is annual
  • A data validation plan is submitted at self-nomination and cannot change after approval
  • Validation audits run before data is submitted
  • Ask vendors for evidence, not assurances
