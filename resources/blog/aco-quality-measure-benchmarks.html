ACO Quality Measure Benchmarks for 2026

How benchmarks work, what changed to flat benchmarks, and how to read your own position.

Blog · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Blog

Two kinds of benchmark

  • Historical benchmarks come from past performance across clinicians and groups and are posted each year in the QPP Resource Library.
  • Flat benchmarks are fixed performance thresholds. CMS has applied them to Medicare CQMs, finalized them for all Medicare CQM measures from 2027, and applied them retroactively to Quality IDs 001, 134 and 236 for 2026.

What the 2026 standard looks like

  • The composite quality score needed to meet the standard is at least 73.85, based on the 40th percentile of MIPS quality category scores.
  • A third-party summary lists an eCQM benchmark for Quality ID 001 of 23.31% or lower (it is an inverse measure) and no eCQM benchmarks for Quality IDs 112 and 113 in 2026.
  • Benchmarks for the claims-based measures are posted after the submission period.

Using benchmarks well

  1. Line up your current rate against the benchmark for each collection type you could use.
  2. Remember that only the best-scoring collection type counts for each measure.
  3. Treat inverse measures carefully: a lower rate is better.
  4. Rerun the comparison after each monthly data refresh.
Do not plan on last year's targetsMoving to flat benchmarks changes what a good rate is. Re-baseline before you set practice goals.

Quality benchmarks are separate from the financial benchmark that determines shared savings. The companion education article explains how the two meet.

At a glance

  • Historical benchmarks for most eCQMs and MIPS CQMs
  • Flat benchmarks for Medicare CQMs
  • Pass line for PY 2026: composite of at least 73.85
  • No eCQM benchmark for 112 and 113 in 2026
