CMS Data Completeness Rules for ACO Quality Reporting

What the 75% requirement counts, who is in the denominator, and why one missing practice can fail a measure.

Blog · Last checked November 17, 2026

← Blog

The rule

Each measure you submit must meet a 75% data completeness requirement. Roughly, at least three of every four patients who are eligible for the measure must be included in the data you submit. The test is per measure, not for the ACO as a whole.

Who counts as eligible

Collection typeEligible patients
eCQM and MIPS CQMAll payers: patients who interact with ACO providers, not only assigned beneficiaries
Medicare CQMMedicare fee-for-service beneficiaries; the quarterly list includes beneficiaries with at least one primary care service encounter in the list period
Medicare eCQM (from 2027)The ACO's assigned beneficiaries

Why it fails

  • A practice's file is missing or late.
  • A feed drops one location or provider.
  • Patients are excluded by a mapping problem, such as an unmapped local code.
  • Patients from an acquired or new practice are not yet included.
  • The population changed when you changed collection type.

How to monitor

  1. Count eligible patients per practice and measure each month.
  2. Compare included patients with eligible patients and keep the ratio visible.
  3. Set an internal floor above 75% so you have room to act.
  4. Assign an owner to every practice below the floor.

Completeness is about whether patients are present. It says nothing about whether they were counted correctly, so pair it with an accuracy check.

At a glance

  • 75% of eligible patients, per measure
  • eCQMs and MIPS CQMs include all payers
  • Medicare CQMs cover Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries
  • Check it monthly, practice by practice
